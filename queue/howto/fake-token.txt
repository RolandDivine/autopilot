Hey, Kobo here. Scammers launch fake versions of popular tokens every day. Same name, same logo, worthless. Here's how to check a token before you buy, in six steps.

Step one. Get the real contract address from the project's official site or CoinGecko. Never from a comment, a DM, or a group chat.

Step two. Compare it, character by character, with the token you're about to buy. Same name means nothing. Only the contract address counts.

Step three. Open the token on a block explorer, like Etherscan or BscScan. Check the holders tab. If a few wallets own most of the supply, be careful.

Step four. Run it through a free token scanner. They flag things like honeypot code, which lets you buy but not sell, or hidden taxes on every sale.

Step five. Check liquidity. If the trading pool is tiny or not locked, the creator can pull it and the price goes to zero.

Step six. Search the token name plus the word scam. Someone may have already been hit.

If you can't verify it, don't buy it. Education, not financial advice. Subscribe for more.
