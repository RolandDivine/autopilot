## 1. Refund scams: image li2.jpg
Got a "Binance refund" message this week?

Don't click it. There is no refund form.

Since the 29 October hack, the messages have been everywhere:

"Claim your compensation."
"Your account is at risk. Verify now."
"Move your coins to this safe wallet."

SMS. WhatsApp. Accounts with thousands of followers and a yellow logo.

All fake.

Every big hack has a second attack. The first hits the exchange. The second hits you.

What's real:
→ Binance announced no claims process. It says losses are covered from its own reserves.
→ No real support agent DMs you first.
→ Nobody legitimate asks for your seed phrase, password or 2FA code.
→ A "safe wallet" someone gives you is their wallet.

The rule I give my own family: if a message makes you rush, stop. Urgency is the scam.

Repost this for the one person in your contacts who keeps everything on an exchange.

## 2. AI trading bots: image li3.jpg
3% a day sounds small.

It's actually ₦4.8 billion.

Put ₦100,000 into an "AI trading bot" promising 3% daily. Compound it for one year and you'd have about ₦4.8 billion.

The best hedge funds on earth are happy with 15–20% a year.

So when a platform says "AI-powered trading, 2–5% daily, withdraw anytime", that isn't a breakthrough. It's an old Ponzi wearing a new word.

How these usually play out:
→ Early users get paid from new users' deposits.
→ Referral bonuses turn victims into recruiters.
→ A "withdrawal fee" or "account upgrade" shows up when you try to cash out.
→ Then the site goes quiet.

"AI" is the perfect costume, because nobody can check what the AI is doing.

I build trading technology, and here is the honest truth: no algorithm guarantees returns.

The 10-second test:
1. Guaranteed daily profit? Walk away.
2. Paid more to bring friends? Walk away.
3. Not registered with the SEC? Walk away.

Has anyone pitched you an "AI bot" this year? Tell me what it promised.

## 3. USDT freezes: image li4.jpg
Your "dollar savings" can be frozen.

$1.77 billion of USDT just was, in a few hours.

When Binance was hacked, Tether froze $1.77B of stolen USDT almost immediately. Across issuers, about $2.7B was frozen.

Good news for the victims.

Uncomfortable news for everyone holding USDT as "uncensorable dollars".

USDT and USDC are issued by companies. Those companies can freeze addresses, and they do: for hacks, sanctions and court orders.

That's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to know what you're actually holding:

→ Bitcoin: nobody can freeze it, and nobody can rescue it either.
→ Stablecoins: a steady price, but a company can say no.
→ Naira in a bank: regulated, but exposed to inflation and policy.

Every option is a trade-off. The real mistake is not knowing which one you picked.

Did you know your USDT could be frozen? Yes or no.

#stablecoins #crypto #fintech

## 4. African startup funding: image li5.jpg
80% of Africa's VC money in August went to Nigeria.

So why can't you raise?

Because "Nigeria" mostly meant a handful of very big rounds.

Across Africa in August:
→ The five biggest deals took 84% of all the money.
→ The number of startups raising $100k or more fell 19%.
→ Active investors fell 22%.

The headline says funding is back: more than $2 billion by mid-September.

For most early founders, it isn't.

The first cheque, the one that takes you from idea to proof, is harder to get than it was a year ago.

What I'm seeing smart founders do instead:
