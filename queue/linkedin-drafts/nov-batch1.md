## 1. Binance hack: image li1.jpg
The biggest crypto theft in history didn't need a genius hack.

It needed one key.

On 29 October, about $6.3 billion left Binance's wallets across ten blockchains, most of it within about 20 minutes. Investigators have linked it to North Korea's TraderTraitor group.

I build and audit DeFi products. Here's the part that stays with me:

→ No smart contract was broken.
→ Nobody guessed a password.
→ Someone got control of one signing key for a hot wallet, and that key could move everything.

Billions in security budget, and the whole thing came down to one key.

Three lessons for anyone building in crypto (or fintech):

1. Your security is only as strong as your key management. Audits don't protect a key someone else is holding.
2. "Hot wallet" means "online target". Keep in it only what you can afford to lose today.
3. Speed of response matters. Stablecoin issuers froze billions within hours. Plan who you'd call before you need to.

Binance says it is covering user losses. That's the good news.

The bad news? The scam wave aimed at ordinary users has already started. I'll cover that tomorrow.

Builders: who holds the keys in your product, and what happens if one leaks?

## 2. The post-hack scam wave: image li2.jpg
There is no Binance refund form.

I have to say it plainly, because since the 29 October hack the messages have started:

"Binance compensation: claim your refund."
"Your account is at risk. Verify now."
"Move your coins to this safe wallet."

Some come by SMS. Some come on WhatsApp. Some come from accounts with thousands of followers and a yellow logo.

All fake.

After every big hack there's a second attack. The first one hits the exchange. The second one hits you.

What the real thing looks like:
→ Binance announced no claims process. Losses are being covered from its own reserves.
→ No real support agent will DM you first.
→ Nobody legitimate will ever ask for your seed phrase, password or 2FA code.
→ A "safe wallet" someone else gives you is their wallet.

The rule I give my family: if a message makes you rush, stop. Urgency is the scam.

Open the official app yourself. Never through a link someone sent you.

Please share this with one person who keeps money on an exchange. That one share could save someone's savings.

Have you received one of these messages yet?

## 3. AI trading bots: image li3.jpg
If an AI trading bot could really make 3% a day, why is it selling itself to you?

Let's do the maths.

3% a day, compounded for one year, turns ₦100,000 into roughly ₦4.8 billion.

The best hedge funds in the world are happy with 15–20% a year.

So when a platform promises "AI-powered trading, 2–5% daily returns, withdraw anytime", it isn't a breakthrough. It's an old Ponzi wearing a new word.

How these schemes usually work:
→ Early users get paid with new users' deposits.
→ Referral bonuses turn victims into recruiters.
→ A "withdrawal fee" or "account upgrade" appears when you try to cash out.
→ Then the site goes quiet.

"AI" is the perfect costume for a Ponzi, because most people can't check what the AI is doing.

I build trading technology. Here's the honest truth: no algorithm guarantees returns. Anyone who guarantees daily profit is either lying or about to learn a very expensive lesson with your money.

The 10-second test:
1. Guaranteed returns? Walk away.
2. Paid more to bring friends? Walk away.
3. Not registered with the SEC? Walk away.

Who in your circle needs to see this before Friday's "investment opportunity"?

## 4. USDT freezes: image li4.jpg
Tether froze $1.77 billion of USDT within hours of the Binance hack.

Is that good news or bad news?

Both.

Good: billions in stolen money got stuck. Across issuers, about $2.7 billion was frozen. That's real money the hackers can't spend.

Uncomfortable: it proves your stablecoins can be frozen too.

A lot of people in Nigeria hold USDT as their "dollar savings". It feels like crypto, so it feels uncensorable.

It isn't. USDT and USDC are issued by companies. Those companies can freeze addresses, and they do, usually because of hacks, sanctions or court orders.

That isn't a reason to panic. It's a reason to understand what you're holding:

→ Bitcoin: nobody can freeze it, and nobody can rescue it.
→ Stablecoins: stable value, but a company can say no.
→ Naira in a bank: regulated, but exposed to inflation and policy.

Every option has a trade-off. The mistake is not knowing which one you've chosen.

Where do you keep your "safe" money, and did you know it could be frozen?

## 5. African startup funding: image li5.jpg
African startup funding is back. Just not for most founders.

The headline says African startups raised more than $2 billion by mid-September.

Look closer.

In August, the five biggest deals took 84% of all the money.
The number of startups raising at least $100k fell 19%.
The number of active investors fell 22%.
Nigeria took about 80% of August's funding, mostly in a handful of large rounds.

So if you're an early-stage founder wondering why every pitch ends in "keep us posted", it isn't just you.

The money is concentrating at the top. The first cheque, the one that gets you from idea to proof, is getting harder to find.

What I'm seeing founders do instead:
→ Apply for grants and fellowships, not just VC
→ Ship with less, using AI tools and smaller teams
→ Get to revenue earlier, so the first cheque comes from customers

This is why I'm putting together $10,000+ worth of support for early founders: grant opportunities, programmes and introductions you can apply for. More on that soon.
