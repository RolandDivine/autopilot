## 6. Exchange vs your own wallet: image li6.jpg
$4.72 billion walked out of one exchange in two days.

People finally asked the right question: "Where is my crypto, really?"

After the Binance hack, users pulled a net $4.72B in the first two days withdrawals reopened.

Some of that was panic. A lot of it was people learning what "not your keys, not your coins" means.

The honest trade-off:

On an exchange
→ Easy, and a big exchange can cover losses
→ But you wait when it's hacked or frozen

In your own wallet
→ Nobody can freeze it
→ Nobody can rescue it either. Lose the seed phrase and it's gone.

And the hardware wallet you just ordered? Buy it from the official store only. Wallets bought from resellers have been caught tampered with.

My rule: trading money on an exchange, savings in a wallet I control.

Where do you keep yours? Exchange, wallet, or both?

#crypto #selfcustody #Nigeria

## 7. A record year for hacks: image li7.jpg
$9.2 billion stolen from crypto this year.

And $6.5 billion of it went in October alone.

That's CertiK's count: 89 incidents in October, the worst month on record. The biggest was the Binance hot-wallet hack.

But look at how the rest happened:
→ Website domains hijacked, so the real URL served a fake site
→ Malicious code slipped into a JavaScript package
→ Stolen keys, not broken maths

Almost none of this was "the blockchain got hacked".

It was people, passwords, keys and websites.

Which is good news, because those are things you can protect:
→ Type the URL yourself and bookmark it
→ Use a hardware wallet for anything you can't afford to lose
→ Revoke old token approvals
→ Never sign something you don't understand

Builders: what's the one security habit you wish every user had?

## 8. Nigeria's licensing reality: image li8.jpg
Nigeria is one of the biggest crypto markets in the world.

Number of fully licensed crypto exchanges there: zero.

On 29 October, the SEC confirmed that no crypto operator has completed full registration yet.

Its draft rules propose ₦2 billion in minimum capital for exchanges.

So millions of people use platforms that are, at best, "in the process".

What that means for you:
→ If something goes wrong, there's no Nigerian regulator you can easily escalate to.
→ "We're registered" in an ad can mean many things. Ask: full registration or provisional?
→ Spread your risk. Don't keep everything on one platform.

And one more thing creators should know: under SEC rules, promoting crypto platforms to Nigerians requires approval first. Penalties start at ₦10 million.

It's a lesson for every fast-growing market, not just Nigeria: adoption moves faster than rules.

Where you live, is your crypto exchange fully licensed? Do you actually know?

#Nigeria #crypto #regulation

## 9. Quantum risk to Bitcoin: image li9.jpg
6.26 million bitcoin have a problem most holders have never heard of.

Their public keys are already visible on the blockchain.

Today, that's fine. Nobody can work backwards from a public key to the private key.

A large enough quantum computer, in theory, could.

That machine doesn't exist yet. Nobody serious thinks your BTC is at risk this year.

But the planning has started:
→ The US standards body NIST has finalised its post-quantum plan.
→ Bitcoin developers are debating how and when to migrate.
→ Coins on older address types are the most exposed.

What I'd do as an ordinary holder:
→ Don't panic-sell over quantum headlines.
→ Avoid reusing addresses.
→ Follow the upgrade discussion. When a migration path ships, move early.

The real risk isn't quantum. It's being the last to update.

Do you think quantum will ever really threaten Bitcoin? Yes, no, or not in our lifetime?

#bitcoin #quantumcomputing #crypto

## 11. When AI tools turn on you: image li11.jpg
For about 11 hours, a fake "Claude Code" extension sat in Cursor's marketplace.

Developers installed it, thinking it was official.

Cursor's security notice says it sent out user and machine details and added an SSH key, a back door into the developer's computer.

This is the new attack surface: the AI tools we trust most.

Nigeria's IT agency, NITDA, warned in October that AI business agents can be hijacked too.

If you build with AI tools:
→ Check the publisher before you install any extension.
→ Look in your SSH authorized_keys file for keys you don't recognise.
→ Rotate tokens and passwords from a clean device if you're unsure.
→ Treat AI agents like new employees: least access, logged actions.

Convenience is the bait. Access is the prize.

Do you check the publisher before installing extensions? Honestly.

#cybersecurity #AI #developers

## 12. Voice-clone scams: image li12.jpg
"Mum, I've been in an accident. Please send money now."

It was her daughter's voice.

It wasn't her daughter.

In October, a mother in California lost $5,400 to a fake kidnapping call made with an AI-cloned voice.

All a scammer needs is a few seconds of audio: a voice note, a TikTok, a YouTube clip.

Then the script is always the same:
→ Panic: "I'm in trouble."
→ Secrecy: "Don't tell anyone."
→ Speed: "Send it now."

The defence costs nothing: a family code word.

One word that only your family knows. If the "emergency" caller can't say it, hang up and call the person back on the number you already have.

Set it up this weekend. It's the cheapest security you'll ever buy.

Does your family have a code word? Yes or no.

## 13. Deepfakes and Nigeria's 2027 election: image li13.jpg
The 2027 election campaign will be the first in Nigeria fought with cheap, convincing deepfakes.

NITDA is already warning about it.

In October, Nigeria's IT agency used Cybersecurity Awareness Month to warn about voice cloning and deepfakes. Its director-general said AI misinformation could be used to manipulate voters.

We've already seen how fast a fake political voice note can travel.

Here's my 3-step check before you forward any shocking clip:
1. Source: who posted it first? A real outlet, or an account that's three days old?
2. Signs: lips out of sync, flat breathing, odd pauses, strange background noise.
3. Search: if it's true, credible outlets will have it within hours. If only WhatsApp has it, wait.

Forwarding a fake is how misinformation wins. Pausing is how it loses.

Will you check before you forward in 2027? Tag someone who forwards everything.

#Nigeria #deepfake #elections

## 15. One founder, many agents: image li15.jpg
I run a content operation that posts daily across Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube.

The team is me and a set of AI agents.

That's the "solo founder" shift everyone is talking about. Even Founder Institute now calls itself an "AI-native company builder".

Here's what my agents actually do:
→ Research trends and draft scripts
→ Design carousels and covers
→ Voice and edit videos
→ Post on schedule and report what worked

Here's what they don't do:
→ Decide what's worth saying
→ Judge if a fact is true
→ Take responsibility when something breaks

The hard truth from building it: automation multiplies judgement. It doesn't replace it. My first week of "fully automated" posting was my worst week.

The founders who win this decade won't have the most agents. They'll have the best judgement about what to delegate.

What would you build if you had a team of 10 AI agents?
