Hey, Kobo here. On July nineteenth, 2024, airports, banks, hospitals, and TV stations went down around the world. Screens showed the blue screen of death.
It wasn't a hack. It was a faulty software update from CrowdStrike, a security company. Their software runs deep inside Windows. One bad file made about eight and a half million Windows computers crash, according to Microsoft.
Flights were cancelled. Payments failed. Many machines had to be fixed one by one, by hand.
What's the lesson for the rest of us? One: when so many systems depend on the same company, one mistake spreads everywhere. Two: updates should roll out slowly, to a few machines first. Three: always have a backup plan. Offline copies of important files, and a way to keep working when the tech fails.
Backups are boring until the day they save you. See ya.
