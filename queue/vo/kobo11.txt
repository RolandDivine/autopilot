Hey, it's Kobo. Ada's cousin Femi designs logos for clients abroad. Getting paid used to take days and cost a lot in fees. Now his clients pay him in a stablecoin.
A stablecoin is a crypto token built to stay at one dollar. The company behind it holds reserves, like cash and short-term government bonds, to back each coin.
So Femi gets paid in minutes, and the value doesn't swing like Bitcoin.
But stable doesn't mean risk-free. The company keeps the interest from those reserves, not Femi. And if the reserves have a problem, the coin can slip below a dollar. It has happened before.
Femi's rules. Use the big, regulated stablecoins. Check the fees before you cash out. And don't leave large amounts sitting on an exchange for months.
A stablecoin is a tool for moving money. It isn't a savings account.
