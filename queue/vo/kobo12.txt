Hey, it's Kobo. Ada has six hundred dollars to invest. Does she put it all in today?
Her friend Tunde tries to time the market. He waits for the perfect dip. The dip comes, he waits for a lower one. Prices go up. He never buys.
Ada does something boring instead. One hundred dollars, on the same day, every month, for six months. When prices are high, her hundred buys less. When prices are low, it buys more.
That's called dollar cost averaging. It doesn't promise profit. But it takes away the stress of guessing, and stops you from putting everything in at the top.
Two rules. Only invest money you won't need for years. And pick something you understand, not whatever is trending on social media this week.
Time in the market usually beats timing the market.
