# Story guide (Roland, 24 Oct 2026: "use what you have learnt as a guide for everything we build or publish from now on")

Every post, Reel, video, carousel, LinkedIn post, landing page and pitch runs through this guide before it ships.
Sources: Startup Secrets "Storytelling" (ABC STORY framework, from the *Seize Your Story!* workshop, Harvard i-Lab / HBS Foundry),
https://startupsecretssandbox.com/storytelling-215 — summarised in our own words; and the "Every founder needs to understand" chart
(studio/reference/sell-the-desire.png). Our existing rules still win where they're stricter (no invented stories, crypto = education
not financial advice, no fake urgency).

## 1. Sell the desire, not the product ("same game, different packaging")
People don't buy features; they buy the feeling or outcome underneath. Name the audience first, then the desire you're really serving.

| If the audience is… | …the real desire is | How it shows up for us (honest version) |
|---|---|---|
| Broke / just starting (most young Nigerians we serve) | **hope** | "You can start with ₦0 and a phone" — real, specific first steps; never get-rich promises |
| Ambitious / big egos | **status** | "Be the one in your group who knows this", skills that make you stand out at work |
| Busy / rich | **time** | "This AI tool does it in 2 minutes" |
| Wealthy / holding crypto | **security** | scam protection, seed phrase, hardware wallets |
| Parents | **peace** | protect family from voice-clone and SIM-swap scams |
| Lonely / isolated builders | **belonging** | "join a community of builders", replying to every comment |
| Healthy / comfortable | **fear (of losing it)** | the address-poisoning formula: a threat they didn't know about |
| Kids/students | **dreams** | what tech careers can become |

Guardrails: we sell real outcomes only. No miracle claims, no fear we can't back with a source, no fake scarcity, no "guaranteed" returns.
Fear is used to protect people, then immediately paired with steps that give them control.

## 2. ABC — design backwards (before writing anything)
- **A · Act:** the ONE action we want from this audience on this piece (follow, save, send to a friend, comment an answer, join the
  community, try the product, book Roland). If there's no ask, rethink why we're posting it.
- **B · Believe:** what must they believe for that action to feel smart, safe and exciting? (e.g. "this scam could hit me", "I can
  learn this", "Roland actually builds this stuff").
- **C · Care:** why it matters in THEIR world: what they lose if nothing changes, how it touches their money, job, family, status.
  Emotion first, then logic.

## 3. STORY — deliver forwards (the order the audience feels it)
- **S · Stage:** open on a vivid scene they can picture themselves in (the old normal and the moment it breaks). The viewer is the
  hero, or someone they relate to. Show what's at stake. This is our first 3 seconds.
- **T · Tension:** make the pain real: it gets worse or stays stuck; alternatives fail. No tension = a report, not a story.
- **O · Opportunity:** Roland (or Kobo) enters as the **guide**, not the hero, with a turning-point insight. Transformation, not features.
  Say why Roland is credible (builder, auditor) in one line.
- **R · Resolution:** proof: numbers, sources, results, a real example. If it's early (our products), paint a vivid, honest picture of
  what's possible.
- **Y · Yes!:** end on who THEY become ("you'll never get drained by a copied address again"), then ONE clear, easy next step.

Mapping: Care → Stage + Tension · Believe → Opportunity + Resolution · Act → Yes.

## 4. Pitfalls we check for on every piece
1. Roland as the hero (bragging about features or himself) — make the audience the hero.
2. Rushing past tension — feel the problem before the fix.
3. Feature-dumping — sell the transformation.
4. Vague asks ("thoughts?", "let me know") — one specific next step.
5. Forgetting it's about them — the final line is about the viewer's win.

## 5. Quick checklist (paste into every script/caption review)
- [ ] Audience named → desire chosen (table above)
- [ ] One action (A), one belief (B), one reason to care (C) written down first
- [ ] First line = Stage with the viewer's stake (≤7 words on screen for Reels)
- [ ] Tension before the solution
- [ ] Roland = guide; one credibility line max
- [ ] Proof: number, source or real example
- [ ] Ends on the viewer's transformation + one clear action
- [ ] Guardrails: honest claims, sources, no invented stories, crypto not financial advice

## 6. By platform
- **Instagram Reels:** S (0–3 s) → T (3–12 s) → O (12–20 s) → R (20–35 s) → Y (last 5 s: "send this to…").
- **Carousels:** slide 1 = Stage, 2–3 = Tension, 4–5 = Opportunity, 6 = Resolution/proof, last = Yes + action.
- **LinkedIn (builder lane):** open on the customer's or the market's pain (not "I built X"), tension, the insight that led Roland to
  build, proof/progress, then the reader's win + one ask (try it, comment, DM). Products are sold through the customer's story.
- **YouTube long-form:** hook = Stage + stakes; chapters walk Tension → Opportunity → Resolution; close on Yes + next video.
- **Landing pages / pitches / "Work with Roland":** same order; the visitor is the hero; one primary button.
