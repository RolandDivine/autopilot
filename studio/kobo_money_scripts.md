## Part 3: Make your money work
Hey, it's Kobo. Remember Ada's melting ice block? Today, she makes her money work.
My grandpa had a mango tree. Every year, mangoes. He never ate the tree. He ate the mangoes.
That's investing. You buy the tree, not just the mangoes.
So Ada puts her money into things that grow, or pay her. A money market fund that pays interest. Government treasury bills. Shares in companies she actually understands.
Now the magic part. Say her money grows ten percent a year. Year one, 240,000 naira becomes 264,000. Year two, she earns on the 264,000, not just the 240. Interest on interest. That's called compounding, and time is its fuel.
Three rules. Understand what you're buying. Spread it out. Be patient. And never put money you need tomorrow into something that can drop today.
Next time: where do big companies get their money from? From people like you. See ya.

## Part 4: The capital market
Hey, it's Kobo. Ever wondered how a small business becomes a giant?
Meet Chidi. He makes the best chin chin in Lagos. Everybody wants it. But to grow, he needs a big factory, and the bank wants too much interest.
So Chidi goes to the capital market. That's where companies and governments raise money from people like you and me, for the long term.
Option one: Chidi sells small pieces of his company, called shares. Buy one, and you own a tiny piece of the chin chin empire. If the company grows, your share can grow too, and you may get a cut of the profits. That's called a dividend.
Option two: he borrows from the public with bonds. You lend him money, he pays you interest, then pays you back.
In Nigeria, this happens on the Nigerian Exchange, watched by the SEC. Every country has its own version, but the idea is the same everywhere.
Remember, shares can go down too. Learn before you buy.
Next time, the most important episode: how to spot an investment scam before it eats your money. See ya.

## Part 5: Spot the scam
Hey, it's Kobo. Last episode, and it might save you a lot of money.
My cousin Emeka got a WhatsApp message. Put in 100,000 naira, get 200,000 back in thirty days. Guaranteed. His friends were already getting paid.
Month one, Emeka got paid too. So he put in more, and brought his sister. Month three, the group went quiet. The admin disappeared.
Here's what really happened. The early people were paid with the new people's money. That's called a Ponzi scheme, and it always collapses.
Four red flags. One: guaranteed high returns. Real investments can go down. Two: pressure to hurry. Three: you earn by bringing in other people. Four: they're not registered with the regulator.
Before you invest anywhere, check that the company is registered. In Nigeria, that's the SEC.
If it sounds too good to be true, it is.
So: save it, grow it, protect it. That's Kobo's money story. Share it with someone who needs it. See ya.
