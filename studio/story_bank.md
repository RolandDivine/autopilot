# Story bank

True stories from Roland, told in his words, for 17:00 stories and lesson hooks. Public repo: only add what he's happy to post.

Format: `- YYYY-MM-DD · topic · what happened (his words) · used: <date or no>`

- 2026-10-12 · crypto origin · 2019, 300 level at the University of Benin. Heard a friend talking about "trading digital assets", asked what it was because he's curious about new tech. His friend explained it; he researched it and got hooked on futures markets. As a student, the hook was extra income, money to do things he couldn't afford. It all clicked after heavy research: books, materials, following people, YouTube. It made sense fast because he'd already learned about the stock market from his grandpa. Seven years in now; it had its pitfalls, but he's glad he made the decision. · used: no
- 2026-10-12 · grandpa & first trade · His grandpa had a company. He showed young Roland the stock pages in the newspaper (that's how people checked prices then). He pointed to a Nigerian company (Roland doesn't remember the name) that had just listed an IPO and said he and his friends would likely get the stocks. Roland never traded stocks himself. His first crypto was Bitcoin, but it was too expensive per coin, so he bought Ethereum. Those were his first two coins. He lost a little at first, then had "beginner's luck" and made close to $500. · used: no
- 2026-10-12 · the liquidation ("my black swan") · KEEP VAGUE: never give the amount and never say whose money it was; just "a lot of borrowed money". After the beginner's luck, he traded with borrowed money and grew it 30% overnight. Then on a Saturday morning between 11 and 12, Bitcoin crashed. At exactly 12 PM he got the liquidation call from Binance: his whole account was gone. He was on 100x leverage. Cause, in his words: greed. He wanted to turn a big number into an even bigger one. "That scattered the entire trajectory of my crypto life." What he does now: never trades with borrowed money, works on his "greed index", trades more like an institution, and knows the risk and reward before every trade. · used: no
- 2026-10-12 · the comeback · Telling the people he owed was hard, but he did it. He then stayed away from crypto and his laptop for about 3 months. It was a heavy psychological burden: he lost weight and looked unwell while writing exams. After the break he came back slowly: learning, demo trading, learning, demo trading, studying trading educators Sam Sticks and Anton Creel (his spelling; possibly Anton Kreil). For a whole year he didn't trade with real money because he was scared of the market. Then he eventually bounced back. · used: no
- 2026-10-13 · AI in his daily life · "I'm a creative. My mind is running a whole planet on its own in my head," so he uses AI to keep everything organised. On Sunday night he talks his week through with his AI agents: what's planned, how far each project has got. The agents are linked to his calendar, email and messages, and they nudge him: reply to this person, get this done, fix this meeting. After a meeting they give him a summary. They also report on the products and infrastructure he's building, with performance updates and test scripts. Tools: Claude, Claude Code, Boardy Pro AI (he is on their board), GPT, Cursor, and Z-code alongside Ollama. He uses GPT for job search, with a scheduled automation that finds crypto content gigs. He couldn't find a good AI tool to manage his socials, and he was spending 2–3 hours a day on X and LinkedIn replies, so he built his own (private for now; he may open it up for people to test). His view: GPT often gets things wrong, and so does Claude when he doesn't do his part. "AI is an efficiency tool, not a creative or foundational tool." You have to know the skill well and know how to prompt. Most people let AI do the real work and the result is mediocre. "You have to be the intelligent one, the creative. Come up with the initiative, then use AI to enhance your efficiency." · used: 2026-10-15 (17:00 story)
- 2026-10-13 · six unlaunched products · He has six products he hasn't launched yet (don't name them). Most are efficiency tools, for government, corporate, private and personal use: "I build a lot of efficiency tools that make people's lives easier and better." - 2026-10-13 · why Klaerus exists · It started with a gap in his own trading: "I was right on the direction of the market, but wrong on timing." Either he got in too early and expected the move to last longer, or he got in too late. Often it was just market manipulation and the way institutions shape market structure. As an institutional-style trader, his stronghold was risk management and exposure: you know the risk and the reward and how to calculate them (Kelly criterion and other quant methods). Retail traders don't. They might set a take-profit price, but will the market have the momentum to reach it? Will it dip into their stop loss first and then go up? Cutting profits short to cope isn't sustainable. He also ran a trading community of close to 200 people, and they kept complaining about the same thing. So he researched further and learned quant trading and the maths behind it. Trading on Polymarket added the last piece: he wanted to predict on a probability scale, with a price attached, and in control. "Why do I have to see Bitcoin is going in this direction in the next five minutes, and it's not properly priced?" That is what gave birth to Klaerus. · used: no
- 2026-10-13 · myths he keeps correcting (Lagos / Africa) · Crypto myths: "crypto is a scam" (especially from educated people in their 50s and 60s in the African region, "so disgusting sometimes"); "it's a get-rich-quick scheme"; or the opposite, "you can't make tangible money from it"; "you don't need to know much or put in effort": put $100 in a rising coin and it becomes $10,000. "No, it doesn't work like that." AI myths: that AI replaces your intelligence, ideas or solutions; "AI will take my job". His view: AI takes the jobs of people who aren't efficient, results-driven or showing initiative. Money myth: "you need a lot of money to start crypto or AI." No: you need the right knowledge, and investing as a whole doesn't need much money to start. METAPHOR (his): crossing an ocean. You can swim, or AI gives you a canoe; better AI gives you a flying boat, or a submarine. But you had the idea to cross, and you have to get in and steer. The submarine doesn't move by itself. He also says the truest knowledge is hard to find and often shared quietly by the right people, not shouted on social media. (Use carefully: frame it as "most free info online is noise; learn from people with real experience", not as "secret info".) · used: no
